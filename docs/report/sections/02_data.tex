\section{Task, data and local evaluation}\label{sec:data}

The predictor outputs the probability that a subject succeeds on an item. A subject is an AI system defined by its model, evaluation harness, harness version and reasoning effort; the items come from benchmarks in the competition's private test pool \citep{truong2026paiec}. Codabench \citep{xu2022codabench} scores a draw of subject--benchmark pairs. Each pair's items are split in half, an acquisition stream from which labels can be acquired and an evaluation set that is scored after budgets of $b \in \{0, 1, 3, 7, 15, 31\}$ acquired labels. With $B_b$ the pair's Brier score \citep{brier1950} at budget $b$, the pair's Brier ALC (area under the learning curve) is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{ALC} = 0.1\,B_0 + 0.2\,(B_1 + B_3 + B_7 + B_{15}) + 0.1\,B_{31},
  \label{eq:alc}
\end{equation}
and the score of a submission is the mean over pairs. Lower is better. At each budget the predictor receives the labels acquired so far for every pair in the draw (none at budget 0), so when a draw puts several subjects on one benchmark, it can use the other subjects' labels. None of my uploads included a labeling policy, and the platform acquired labels at random. Each upload gets formative feedback: its score on one draw of 8 or 9 pairs, drawn afresh for every upload, with per-budget Brier tables.

\paragraph{Data.}
I trained and tested on the public measurement-db \citep{measurementdb2026} at revision \texttt{bc8204d8}; the only other training data are the LLM difficulty ratings and subject guesses described in Section~\ref{sec:method}. Because the competition scores binary outcomes, I kept an item only if its declared response scale is $\{0,1\}$. This drops MMDocRAG \citep{dong2025mmdocrag}, graded on a 26-level scale, and 271 MathArena items graded on an interval scale. I filtered on the declared scale rather than on the scores that occur, because 222 of those 271 items have only 0 and 1 recorded and would otherwise have stayed in. What is left is 10,361 items, 229 subjects and 225,764 responses on five benchmarks: MathArena \citep{balunovic2025matharena}, Multi-SWE-bench \citep{zan2025multiswe}, REAL \citep{garg2025real}, ResearchCodeBench \citep{hua2025researchcodebench} and SWE-rebench \citep{badertdinov2025swerebench} (Table~\ref{tab:data}).

\paragraph{Local evaluation.}
To compare predictors offline I wrote a harness that simulates the hosted protocol on the public data. Each fold holds out one benchmark: the predictor is fit on the other four and scored on pairs from the held-out one. Each subject with enough items on the held-out benchmark forms a pair, whose items are shuffled with a seeded generator and split in half. Labels are acquired in rounds, so no pair gets a second label until every pair has its first, and likewise at 3, 7, 15 and 31. The harness picks items with the default random rule of the competition's starting kit, copied line for line from the kit's streaming client, and at each budget scores the predictor on every recorded response of the evaluation items. Appendix~\ref{app:harness} gives the remaining details.

I checked my scoring code against Codabench in two ways. A constant 0.5 scores exactly 0.25 on every pair, locally and on Codabench, as it must with 0/1 labels. Recomputing each pair's ALC from the per-budget Brier tables of my Codabench feedback reproduces the reported value to within $10^{-6}$, the rounding of the tables, for all 68 pair results in the feedback files I kept (the seven uploads of Table~\ref{tab:formative} and the probe 968649). Neither check tests the split, the acquisition order or the labels passed to the predictor. For those I rely on the rules page and the kit.

I compare predictors in three settings. The \emph{default} setting follows the rules' minimum of 80 items per pair and caps pairs at 1,000 items, the platform's limit for a whole draw; it gives 192 pairs per seed, with 265 items on average. The \emph{hidden-sized} setting imitates the smaller pairs of a Codabench draw, where 8 or 9 pairs share at most 1,000 subject--item combinations: it keeps at most 400 random items of the held-out benchmark and pairs with at least 60, about 157 pairs per seed with 153 items on average. For the \emph{rated} setting, which tests the LLM inputs, I kept only items with an LLM rating and pairs with at least 40 of them (198 pairs per seed). I lowered the minimum below the rules' 80 in the last two settings to keep enough pairs. In any setting a pair can also be given the labels of $K$ partner pairs on the same benchmark, and I give $K$ with each result.

All runs use seeds 0, 1 and 2, which set the split, the stream order and the partners but not the fitted predictor. A comparison reports $\Delta = \mathrm{ALC}(\text{candidate}) - \mathrm{ALC}(\text{reference})$, averaged over the pair results of all seeds matched on seed and pair, so $\Delta < 0$ favors the candidate. Its standard error treats each subject--benchmark pair as one cluster across the seeds (CR0, Appendix~\ref{app:harness}). Only four held-out benchmarks give more than one pair, so these standard errors say little about how a new benchmark would behave.

Every test in Appendix~\ref{app:rules} had its decision rule written into the script before the first run, and committed first except where the appendix says otherwise. The baselines, V1's prior scale of 0.5 and a stress test of V1 not reported here (held-out benchmarks made 1 or 2 logits easier or harder) had no rule fixed in advance. Neither had V3's switch to a scale of 0, which I made from formative feedback although it was worse offline ($+0.00300$ Brier ALC, SE 0.00047, in the default setting and $+0.00459$, SE 0.00055, in the hidden-sized setting with $K=1$). The first run of the V5 test had a bug (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}). It was fixed without changing the rule, and I report only the second run; the invalid run, which tested a different model, had also chosen a guess trust of 0.75 and passed, and it is not in the repository.
