\section{Model details}\label{app:model}

\paragraph{Metadata prior (baseline, V1, V2).}
The baseline's prior $p_0$ and V1's $g(s)$ come from one weighted ridge regression (penalty 5) fitted on the training benchmarks. Its target is each training pair's smoothed logit success rate, $\mathrm{logit}((k + 0.5)/(n + 1))$, where $n$ counts every trial; its weight is the pair's number of distinct items, capped at 200. Its predictors are benchmark fixed effects and standardized subject features clipped at $\pm 3$: the release date as a fractional year, a flag for a missing release date, a flag for ``think'' or ``reason'' in the subject's name or settings, and indicators for the providers with at least three training subjects (13 in the submissions' fit on all five benchmarks, so 16 features; 11 to 13 in the offline folds). A subject's score is the fitted value without benchmark effects plus, for a subject seen in training, its weighted mean residual, shrunk toward 0 by adding 1 to the sum of its weights (normalized to mean 1). The baseline's $p_0$ is the mean over the training benchmarks of $\sigma$ applied to this score plus that benchmark's effect. For V1, $g(s)$ is this score, and $\bar g$ is its mean over the training subjects. The ridge penalty and the shrinkage were set when the baseline was written and not varied in any recorded run; the baseline's prior strength of 4 was the best of 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 on the same held-out folds, in a sweep run on the first version of the baseline, before features that vary within at most one benchmark were dropped. Because V3, V4 and V5 use $\lambda = 0$, none of this affects them.

\paragraph{Prior scale and width (V1).}
V1 used $\lambda = 0.5$, although $\lambda = 1$ was better offline by 0.0016 (SE 0.0004): in a scenario analysis that is not in the repository, the half-strength prior had about a third of the full prior's worst-case loss when metadata do not carry over, with the same average. Two of its scenarios were modeled on the baseline's formative per-budget curve; the choice held without them. V3's $\lambda = 0$ and the choice between V4 and V5 (Section~\ref{sec:results}) were the only choices made directly from formative feedback. I tried one alternative to $s_\theta = 1.5$: at $\lambda = 1$, $s_\theta = 1.75$ scored $+0.0016$ (SE 0.0002) worse, and I kept 1.5.

\paragraph{Two-stage pooling (V2).}
In the first stage, each label $y$ of a partner $s'$ on item $j$ becomes an observation of $z_j$. I take the mean $\mu$ and variance $v$ of $\theta_{s'}$ under V1's posterior given the partner's other labels, so that the label is not counted twice, and model $y \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}\bigl(\sigma((\kappa_0\mu - z_j)/h)\bigr)$ with $h = \kappa(\kappa_0^2 v)$. In the second stage I combine the prior on $z_j$ with these observations on a grid and keep the posterior mean $\hat z_j$ and variance $\omega_j^2$. The target subject's labels on such items enter the likelihood as $\sigma\bigl((2y-1)(\kappa_0\theta - \hat z_j)/\kappa_j\bigr)$ with $\kappa_j = \kappa(\omega_j^2)$, and its other labels enter as in V1. The prediction for item $i^*$ averages $\sigma(\kappa_0\theta - z)$ over the posteriors of $\theta$ and of the item's difficulty. In practice it averages $\sigma\bigl((\kappa_0\theta - \hat z_{i^*})/\kappa_{i^*}\bigr)$ over the posterior of $\theta$, and it is the posterior mean of $\sigma(\theta)$ when no partner has labeled $i^*$.

\paragraph{Grids.}
The posterior of $\theta$ is evaluated on 2,801 points spaced 0.01 apart over $[-14, 14]$, and the posterior of an item difficulty on 1,201 points spaced 0.02 apart over $[-12, 12]$, both with log-sum-exp stabilization.

\paragraph{Rasch difficulties for the ratings.}
The difficulties that the ratings were compared with, and regressed on, come from a Rasch model $\mathrm{logit}\,P(y = 1) = \theta_s - z_j$ fitted separately on each benchmark from all public subjects and responses, every trial counted. It is fitted by penalized joint maximum likelihood with priors $\mathcal{N}(0, 2^2)$ on $z$ and $\mathcal{N}(0, 3^2)$ on $\theta$, using L-BFGS-B, and $z$ is centered to mean 0 on each benchmark. MathArena items are pooled by problem, since its item identifiers differ only by prompt wrapper. \texttt{scripts/build\_rating\_samples.py} refits them and reproduces the saved values exactly. Pooled over the four benchmarks, their variance within a benchmark is about 5.6 (from 3.2 on Multi-SWE-bench to 9.7 on MathArena), against the $\tau^2 = 4$ assumed in Eq.~\eqref{eq:trust}.

\paragraph{Guess calibration (V5).}
The calibration of Eq.~\eqref{eq:guessprior} is fitted on the subjects with 40 to 1,000 items on each rated benchmark, one point per subject and benchmark, using the logit of the subject's success rate over all its responses there. In the offline test each held-out benchmark gets a fit from the other three: $(a_g, c_g, s_g)$ was $(-0.048, 1.072, 0.695)$ from 117 pairs with MathArena held out, $(0.154, 0.784, 0.848)$ from 150 with Multi-SWE-bench held out, $(0.092, 0.807, 0.773)$ from 172 with REAL held out and $(0.039, 0.830, 0.827)$ from 173 with ResearchCodeBench held out. The submission's calibration uses all four rated benchmarks (204 pairs): $a_g = 0.040$, $c_g = 0.851$, $s_g = 0.811$, which with $t_g = 0.75$ gives the prior $\mathcal{N}(0.030 + 0.639 \operatorname{logit} q_s,\ 1.164^2)$.

\paragraph{Check of subject strength.}
In the check of Section~\ref{sec:method}, the within-benchmark correlation of 0.818 averages 0.957 on MathArena with 0.767, 0.661 and 0.886 on the other three benchmarks. On Multi-SWE-bench, subjects that ran on the Python tasks and those that ran on the other languages share no items, so the check centers both quantities within each of the two groups.
