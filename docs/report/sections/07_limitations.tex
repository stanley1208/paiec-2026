\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Every offline result holds out one of only four public benchmarks with enough pairs, and each formative score is one draw of 8 or 9 pairs. My standard errors treat subject--benchmark pairs as independent; with the four benchmarks as the unit instead, V5's gain over V4 has a standard error near 0.003, not 0.0013.
  \item The ratings and guesses were tested on public benchmarks whose results Claude may have seen in training. In the pre-check, its three-example guesses matched Rasch ability at 0.957 on MathArena, whose per-model results are published (0.749 for the single-example guesses V5 uses). The offline gains may therefore be larger than they would be on new benchmarks, and if the hidden benchmarks are public too, the hosted gains could contain the same effect. I cannot check this.
  \item V4 and V5 depend on a proprietary model behind a paid API. Its replies vary between calls and are not logged on the platform, so the hosted runs cannot be reproduced exactly; the V4 run cost \$9.49, billed. During scoring the submission sends each subject's recorded fields and some item texts to Anthropic.
  \item Offline every item had a rating, and each guess was asked with one of the subject's own items as the example, a rated one for 229 of the 232 guesses (one per subject and benchmark). On Codabench, ratings and guesses that arrive after the 4-second wait are missing for that prediction, items whose text is shared by several problems (270 of MathArena's 636 problems) were never rated offline, and the example for a guess can be a task given only as an image or a bare instruction.
  \item Formative feedback, which comes from the hidden test pool, informed V3's $\lambda = 0$, taken against the offline evidence, and the switch from V4 to V5, taken under a rule committed before the upload. Two scenarios of the analysis behind V1's $\lambda = 0.5$ were also modeled on formative feedback, although the choice held without them.
  \item The pooling gain assumes that each pair's items are split independently, which the rules do not state. The rating trust in Eq.~\eqref{eq:trust} assumes a difficulty variance of $\tau^2 = 4$, while the fitted Rasch difficulties have a variance of about 5.6 within a benchmark (Appendix~\ref{app:model}).
\end{itemize}
